About

I work at the intersection of genomics and computation — building analysis pipelines, applying machine learning to gene expression data, and paying close attention to whether the results actually hold up. My background spans a PhD and MSc in Bioinformatics at the University of West London, alongside teaching on the MSc Bioinformatics programme.

Much of my recent interest is in evaluation methodology: how easily performance figures in small-sample genomic studies can be inflated by routine choices, and what it takes to report them honestly.

Research

PhD Bioinformatics · University of West London · 2026

Deep learning for gene expression classification

Structural, Functional and Genomic Decompensation Analysis of the Brain in Chiari Malformation I Patients

Developed convolutional neural network architectures for classifying brain tissue from microarray gene expression profiles, implemented from first principles in C++. The work included empirically-justified activation function selection via 10×5-fold repeated stratified cross-validation with Friedman and Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc testing, and benchmarking against classical machine learning baselines under a matched evaluation protocol.

In preparation

Evaluation pitfalls in small-sample gene expression classification

A quantified study of how feature-selection leakage and accuracy-only reporting inflate apparent performance on small, imbalanced transcriptomic datasets — measuring the size of the bias under a matched protocol, and showing that architectural depth confers no advantage over logistic regression at this scale.
