01 / about

Most of my work is in transcriptomics: differential expression, classification of tissue from gene expression, and the statistics that decide whether a result is actually there. I work mainly in Python and R, and I care a lot about whether an analysis can be run again by someone who is not me.

I completed my PhD at the University of West London in 2026 and now lead the MSc Bioinformatics programme there, teaching the computational biology modules and supervising student projects.

Lately I have been preoccupied with one problem in particular: how easily a respectable-looking performance figure in a small genomic study can be wrong, and how much work it takes to notice.

02 / what I'm working on

Accuracy can hide a model that does nothing

Gene expression datasets are usually small and rarely balanced. The brain cancer cohort below has 130 samples, 117 of them tumour. A model that simply answers "tumour" every single time is therefore 90% accurate, and completely useless.

I am writing this up alongside a second result: when the classifier genes are chosen using the whole dataset rather than inside each training fold, logistic regression's κ rises from 0.790 to 0.915. Roughly 14% of the headline number was an artefact of the evaluation, not the biology. Both findings come out of re-examining my own thesis pipeline.

03 / doctoral work

PhD Bioinformatics · University of West London · 2022–2026

Neural network models for gene expression classification

Structural, Functional and Genomic Decompensation Analysis of the Brain in Chiari Malformation I Patients

I implemented convolutional and artificial neural networks from first principles in C++, then evaluated them properly: activation functions chosen by 10×5-fold repeated stratified cross-validation with Friedman testing and Bonferroni-corrected post-hoc comparisons, and benchmarked against logistic regression, SVM and random forests under an identical protocol.

The benchmarking is the part I would defend hardest. It is also the part that told me the deep model was not earning its complexity.
